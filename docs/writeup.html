FabTwin: stochastic scheduling and transport optimisation for a semiconductor fab

Ates Parilti · Industrial Engineering, TU Eindhoven · code, data and live demo: github.com/atesparilti1/fabtwin · atesparilti1.github.io/fabtwin

Problem

Wafer fabs are re-entrant flexible job shops: a lot revisits lithography once per mask layer, furnaces process batches of up to 6 lots of the same recipe, scanners lose time on reticle changes, and some steps carry queue-time (Q-time) limits after which a lot must be re-cleaned or scrapped. Lots move on an overhead hoist transport (OHT) network, and tools fail stochastically. FabTwin models the three coupled layers (scheduling, transport, maintenance) and evaluates policies in a discrete-event digital twin under uncertainty.

The benchmark fab (medium-s0, synthetic, SMT2020-inspired, all parameters documented) has 16 tool groups, 55 non-identical machines, 3 products with routes of 58–69 steps and 7–8 litho visits, 20.0 lot starts/day plus hot lots, Weibull failures, lognormal repairs and processing times, 3% litho rework and a 4 h clean→furnace Q-time.

Methods

Exact. A CP-SAT model (optional intervals, circuit constraints for sequence-dependent setups, pairwise batch synchronisation with a cumulative capacity, hard Q-time) and a time-indexed MIP solved with HiGHS give optima and lower bounds on static snapshot instances. Heuristics. Classic rules (FIFO, SPT, EDD, ODD, CR, ATC), setup-aware ATCS, a bottleneck-first decomposition that sequences litho with CP-SAT and dispatches the rest around it, and ALNS with problem-specific operators (batch merge, setup-aware litho resequencing, Q-time repair) on priority vectors decoded by active/non-delay schedule generation. Transport. Vehicles are assigned per decision epoch with the Hungarian algorithm (priority credits for hot and Q-time lots), routed by congestion-aware A*, and booked with headway time-window reservations, which excludes collisions and deadlocks by construction. Maintenance. Age-based PM: the nominal plan minimises per-tool downtime analytically (renewal reward); the robust plan is selected in the stochastic simulator by two-stage screening and selection with common random numbers, then validated on fresh seeds. Simulation methodology. Own event engine; 120-day runs, 30-day warm-up (MSER-5 suggests day 35), 200 replications, CRN via per-entity random streams, paired 95% t-intervals. Little's law holds within 1.4% on average.

Exact vs heuristic

CP-SAT proved optimality on 12/12 small instances (≤100 operations); the time-indexed MIP reached proven optimality on 6 of them and agreed with CP-SAT on all 6. Mean gap to the optimum on instances with a non-zero optimum: best dispatching rule 36.3%, bottleneck-first decomposition 32.5%, ALNS 27.3%. With a 60 s limit CP-SAT still gives the best solutions at 240 operations, but beyond that it fails to find good (or any) solutions, while ALNS keeps improving on the rules, which is the crossover that motivates decomposition and metaheuristics at fab scale.

Fab-level KPIs did not change significantly: a move takes 1.5–2 min against a cycle time of days, and Q-time violations come from furnace queueing.

Trade-offs and robustness checks

Q-time. The FabTwin policy has 104% (± 3%) more Q-time violations than FIFO: due-date- and setup-oriented rules pull lots through the clean step faster than furnaces batch them. A Q-time-aware release gate is the natural next step. Warm-up. Re-running the ATCS-vs-CR comparison with a 40-day warm-up gives -88.8% ± 22.6% weighted tardiness (vs -88.0% ± 21.8% with 30 days). Learned dispatching. Tree GP evolved a priority expression on training seeds; on 200 held-out seeds it beat ATC (-22.5% ± 7.6% weighted tardiness) and CR (-46.7% ± 10.2%) but not the setup-aware ATCS (+345%), so it is not used in any policy.

Limitations

Synthetic fab (SMT2020 structure, not its data); no operator, reticle-availability or engineering-hold constraints; point vehicles at constant speed, stocker-to-tool hand-off instantaneous; failures on calendar time with preempt-resume; static exact models use 0.1 h discretisation; ALNS is limited by its schedule-generation scheme (reported gaps show it). The browser what-if mode is a simplified TypeScript port; all reported numbers come from the Python engine and are regenerated by make bench.
